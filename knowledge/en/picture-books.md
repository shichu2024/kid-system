# Picture Book Library by Age (0-12 Years) — English Mirror

> Version: 1.0 ｜ Updated: 2026-09-08
> Mirror of `knowledge/picture-books.md` (zh source of truth). Chinese titles are kept with their common English editions; books without a widely available English edition are replaced by equally classic English-language titles of the same age band (replacements marked **[substitute]**).
> Aligns with kid-learning six dimensions: reading / language / math cognition / art / general knowledge / habits.

---

## 0. Universal Read-Aloud Principles

1. **Dialogic Reading**: read — ask — wait — respond. Pause 2-3 seconds at key pages, ask open questions ("Where did the fish hide?"), wait for the child to point or speak, then expand on their answer. The most evidence-backed method for ages 1-5 language growth.
2. **Finger-pointing strategy by stage**: 1-2y — point and name objects in pictures; 2-3y — transition to tracking the printed text with your finger so the child links spoken words to print; 3y+ — point to text only when the child shows interest, never force echo-reading.
3. **The value of rereading**: "Read it again!" is a normal, important learning behavior. First pass = plot, second = details, third = prediction ("What does the caterpillar eat next?"). Rereading one book for 1-2 weeks beats a new book every day.
4. **Positioning**: child sits beside you (same view of the page), not opposite; start with 10-15 minute sessions and follow the child's attention.
5. **Books are toys, not textbooks**: chewing, throwing and page-flipping are normal exploration for 0-1y; there is nothing to "master" in a shared reading.

## 1. 0-12 Months: Senses & Attachment

**Selection principles**: ≤20 pages; no plot or minimal plot (one sentence per image); board/cloth/touch-and-feel books that survive chewing; large, high-contrast or gentle-face illustrations; rhythmic text.

**Six-dimension mapping**: this stage is mainly **language** (rhythmic input, naming) and building the **reading** habit (books = pleasure); habit-forming series begin gently.

- **《蹦！》Hop!** (Tadaaki Matsuoka) — no widely circulated English edition; **[substitute] From Head to Toe** (Eric Carle) fills the same action-imitation slot ｜Topic: animal actions ｜Builds: rhythm, action imitation
  - Tip: on every "hop" page, lift the baby gently so sound, motion and picture bind together; for crawlers, put the book down and demonstrate a bounce.
- **《小熊宝宝》Little Bear series** (Yoko Sasaki) — no common English edition; **[substitute] Leslie Patricelli board books** (e.g., *Yummy Yucky*, *Quiet Loud*) ｜Topic: daily routines ｜Builds: habits, routine vocabulary
  - Tip: start with the 2-3 books matching what the baby did today ("Little Bear eats — you ate today too!"); the sound words matter more than plot.
- **《晚安，月亮》Goodnight Moon** (Margaret Wise Brown / Clement Hurd)｜Topic: bedtime ritual ｜Builds: sleep routine, vocabulary
  - Tip: fix it as the last book before sleep, reading "goodnight" lines softer and slower each page; from week two, point from the page to the real lamp and chair in the room.
- **《棕色的熊、棕色的熊，你在看什么？》Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?** (Bill Martin Jr. / Eric Carle)｜Topic: animals & colors ｜Builds: repeated syntax input, color naming
  - Tip: use one fixed rising-question/falling-answer intonation; after a few weeks, pause before each animal name and wait for the baby's "uh" before continuing.
- **《脸，脸，各种各样的脸》Faces** (Ryōhei Yanagihara) — limited English circulation; keep the Chinese edition or use any high-contrast face board book｜Topic: expressions ｜Builds: face preference, earliest emotion recognition
  - Tip: mirror every expression on the page at your baby and hold it for 3 seconds.
- **《抱抱》Hug** (Jez Alborough)｜Topic: attachment ｜Builds: security, physical affection
  - Tip: the book has essentially one word — hug your child tightly each time it appears; the best pick for combining reading with skin-to-skin contact.
- **Cloth & touch-and-feel book buying guide** (no brands)｜Topic: sensory play ｜Builds: tactile input, page-turning practice
  - Tip: choose ①washable fabric with sturdy stitching; ②one built-in sound element (crinkle/rattle) is enough; ③varied textures per page; ④no loose ribbons over ~5 cm. Press the child's hand on each texture and name it: "fuzzy", "smooth".

## 2. 1-2 Years: Language Explosion & First Rules

**Selection principles**: 20-32 pages; one simple plot or pure game structure; short repeated sentences; interactive formats (holes, flaps, search) first; everyday-life topics.

**Six-dimension mapping**: **language** (repetitive-syntax books) + **habits** (boundary stories) + early **math cognition** (dots and counting).

- **《好饿的毛毛虫》The Very Hungry Caterpillar** (Eric Carle)｜Topic: growth & food ｜Builds: counting 1-5, days of week, food naming
  - Tip: have the child poke a finger through each eating hole while counting "one apple, two pears"; end with arms wide: "A butterfly! Let's fly!"
- **《大卫，不可以》No, David!** (David Shannon)｜Topic: behavior boundaries ｜Builds: rule awareness, unconditional acceptance
  - Tip: exaggerated (never scary) "No!" each page, then "What did David do?"; always finish the final "Yes, David... I love you" page with a hug.
- **《我爸爸》《我妈妈》My Dad / My Mum** (Anthony Browne)｜Topic: family ｜Builds: role words, simile patterns
  - Tip: at "as strong as a gorilla", flex your arm and let the child try to push it; close the book with a "My dad/mum is as ___ as ___" round-robin, accepting every answer.
- **《小金鱼逃走了》Where's the Fish?** (Goro Gomi) — English edition has limited circulation; **[substitute] Where's Spot?** (Eric Hill) for the same hide-and-seek mechanic｜Topic: visual search ｜Builds: observation, focus, position words
  - Tip: child must point at the fish before each page-turn, then name the disguise ("hiding in the flowerpot"); later copy the game by hiding a toy at home.
- **《点点点》Press Here** (Hervé Tullet)｜Topic: instruction play ｜Builds: color, quantity, cause & effect
  - Tip: follow the book's instructions exactly — press, shake, blow — so the child believes they command the book; after 3+ reads, swap roles and deliberately obey wrong so the child corrects you.
- **《连在一起》Linked Together** (Taro Miura) — no common English edition; **[substitute] Ten Tiny Toes** by Caroline Jayne Church (rhyming body/affection board book)｜Topic: connection ｜Builds: matching concept, attachment expression
  - Tip: press your face to the child's face on every "linked together" refrain — a whole-book ritual.

## 3. 2-3 Years: Emotions & Imagination

**Selection principles**: 25-35 pages; complete mini-plot (setup-development-resolution); suspense and prediction possible; vivid character emotions; comedy or repetitive structures the child can "shout first".

**Six-dimension mapping**: **language** (predictive questioning) + emotion recognition + **math cognition** (quantity stories) + **habits** (tooth-brushing).

- **《猜猜我有多爱你》Guess How Much I Love You** (Sam McBratney / Anita Jeram)｜Topic: expressing love ｜Builds: comparative structures, emotional language
  - Tip: act it out — "as wide as I can reach" with real arms; then run a "My love is as ___ as ___" contest and accept even "as big as a dinosaur!"
- **《鳄鱼怕怕牙医怕怕》The Crocodile and the Dentist** (Taro Gomi; English edition published by Chronicle)｜Topic: dentist visit & brushing ｜Builds: perspective-taking, brushing motivation
  - Tip: use two distinct voices although both characters say the same words — the child discovers the dentist is scared too; finish the book and walk straight to the bathroom chanting the book's line.
- **《彩虹色的花》The Rainbow-Colored Flower** (art by Michael Grejniec) — no widely available English edition; **[substitute] The Rainbow Fish** (Marcus Pfister) for the sharing/giving theme｜Topic: sharing ｜Builds: generosity, seasons & life cycles
  - Tip: as each petal (or scale) is given away, ask "If it were your petal, who would you give it to?"
- **《母鸡萝丝去散步》Rosie's Walk** (Pat Hutchins)｜Topic: text-picture irony ｜Builds: picture-reading narration, humor
  - Tip: the text is one flat line — let the child narrate the fox's disasters; the best first "child tells, parent listens" book.
- **《首先有一个苹果》First There Is an Apple** (Hiroshi Ito) — no common English edition; **[substitute] 1, 2, 3 to the Zoo** (Eric Carle) for cumulative counting｜Topic: number & quantity ｜Builds: counting 1-10, growth structure
  - Tip: count the old elements before introducing the new one each page ("two foxes chase one bear"); after the book is familiar, build the same sentence with 3 strawberries at the table.
- **《谁藏起来了》Who's Hiding?** (Satoru Onishi) — no common English edition; **[substitute] Dear Zoo** (Rod Campbell) for guess-who flap play; for memory training, any "look and remember" page game works｜Topic: memory & observation ｜Builds: working memory, animal-feature words
  - Tip: ask "Who's hiding?", allow 5 seconds of recall, then turn; wrong answers mean going back one page together to check, not "wrong".
- **From Head to Toe** (Eric Carle)｜Topic: body movement ｜Builds: body-part naming, gross-motor imitation
  - Tip: every Q&A must end with the action — "I am a penguin and I can turn my head. Can you?" — one turn equals one round, doubling as gross-motor play.

## 4. 3-6 Years: Narrative & Worldview Expansion

**Selection principles**: 30-45 pages; multi-thread plots or suspense; "why" discussions possible; science and life topics enter; series begin (pick the entry volume, don't buy the whole set at once).

**Six-dimension mapping**: **general knowledge / science** (school-bus series) + emotion recognition (anger story) + **language** (retelling & prediction) + **habits** and **art** (collage/watercolor styles extend into crafts).

- **《爷爷一定有办法》Something from Nothing** (Phoebe Gilman)｜Topic: making do ｜Builds: prediction, dual-line reading (the mice under the floor)
  - Tip: stop before each cut and ask "What will Grandpa make this time?"; on the second pass, read only the mouse family's subplot.
- **《逃家小兔》The Runaway Bunny** (Margaret Wise Brown / Clement Hurd)｜Topic: unconditional love ｜Builds: if-then patterns, chase imagination
  - Tip: play the "If you become..., I will become..." chain — you always catch up but never win; the story goes wherever the child goes.
- **《活了 100 万次的猫》The Cat That Lived a Million Times** (Yoko Sano) — an English edition exists but circulation is limited; if unavailable, keep the Chinese edition or pair with **[substitute] The Velveteen Rabbit** (Margery Williams) for the love-and-loss theme｜Topic: love & life ｜Builds: first life education, open discussion
  - Tip: after reading ask only two questions — "Was the cat happy before? And later?" — and allow silence or off-topic answers.
- **《不一样的卡梅拉》The Camelia Chickens series** (Christian Jolibois / Christian Heinrich) — French original *Les P'tites Poules*; no common English edition; **[substitute] the "Elephant & Piggie" series by Mo Willems** (start with *Today I Will Fly!*) for a first character series｜Topic: adventure & individuality ｜Builds: long-narrative stamina, humor
  - Tip: with many characters and fast pace, do voiced characters on the first read; later let the child claim one character permanently.
- **《神奇校车》The Magic School Bus (picture-book editions)** (Joanna Cole / Bruce Degen)｜Topic: science ｜Builds: **general knowledge** core (water cycle, human body, Earth)
  - Entry volume: start with *The Magic School Bus at the Waterworks* — it has a full visible cycle, a satisfying "knowledge loop".
  - Tip: the student "notes" in the margins are a second text layer — let the child pick one note to read aloud while you carry the main story.
- **《菲菲生气了》When Sophie Gets Angry—Really, Really Angry** (Molly Bang)｜Topic: anger & self-calming ｜Builds: emotion naming, calming strategy
  - Tip: during the kicking-screaming pages, invite the child to stomp and roar along; then watch together how Sophie "cried a while, looked at the rocks and trees" to cool down; name a family calm corner after "Sophie's tree".
- **《石头汤》Stone Soup** (Jon J. Muth's edition is the best-known English version)｜Topic: cooperation ｜Builds: social cognition, story retelling
  - Tip: as each villager brings one food, have the child recite the growing list ("now the pot has carrots and mushrooms...") — memory and enumeration practice.

## 5. 6-9 Years: Bridge & Early Chapter Books

**Selection principles**: bridge books (~50% picture / 50% text) → thin chapter books of 60-100 pages; one chapter ≈ 15-20 minutes; humor, friendship and adventure enter most easily; "paired reading" (parent one page, child one passage) is the key format.

**Six-dimension mapping**: **language** (independent-reading launch) + **general knowledge** (chapter nonfiction) + social-emotional (friendship titles); keep the "child wants" vs "parent wants" ratio near 3:1 to protect motivation.

- **《青蛙和蟾蜍》Frog and Toad series** (Arnold Lobel)｜Topic: friendship ｜Builds: bridge-book entry, gentle philosophy
  - Entry volume: *Frog and Toad Are Friends*, starting with "Spring" — each story is 5 minutes, one per day.
  - Tip: you read Frog, the child reads Toad (fewer words) — turn-taking lowers the independent-reading barrier.
- **《神奇树屋》Magic Tree House series** (Mary Pope Osborne)｜Topic: time-travel adventure ｜Builds: chapter stamina, history & geography
  - Entry volume: #1 *Dinosaurs Before Dark*; the first four form a complete mini-season before deciding to continue.
  - Tip: every chapter ends on a cliffhanger — close the book there; next day the child retells "where we stopped" before you continue.
- **《一年级大个子二年级小个子》** (Kusatake Furuta) — no common English edition; **[substitute] My Father's Dragon** (Ruth Stiles Gannett), a classic short first chapter book｜Topic: courage & growth ｜Builds: realistic (or gentle fantasy) fiction entry, stamina
  - Tip: paired reading in second half of grade 1; at the protagonist's "afraid of the tree-lined road" moment, swap stories about something the child fears but wants to try.
- **《夏洛的网》Charlotte's Web** (E.B. White)｜Topic: friendship & mortality ｜Builds: first full novel, life education
  - Tip: the first whole-family novel — you read description, the child reads dialogue; warn about Charlotte's ending in advance, and allow sadness after the final page.
- **《没头脑和不高兴》** (Rong Rong) — no English edition; **[substitute] the "Henry and Mudge" series** (Cynthia Rylant) for boy-and-dog everyday short chapters｜Topic: habits & humor ｜Builds: mother-tongue reading fun (zh) / easy independent chapters (en)
  - Tip: each piece is short enough for fully independent reading (~15 min) — the low-threshold bridge from "reading together" to "reading alone".
- **《了不起的狐狸爸爸》Fantastic Mr Fox** (Roald Dahl)｜Topic: wit & family ｜Builds: chapter-book "hook", dark humor
  - Entry tip: the shortest, fastest Roald Dahl — the gateway before *Charlie and the Chocolate Factory*; expand by interest afterwards.

## 6. 9-12 Years: Whole Books & Reflection

**Selection principles**: whole novels (50k+ words) read independently, with "parallel reading" (each reads their own copy, discuss at dinner); fantasy / realistic / nonfiction three tracks; begin comparing translations vs originals and adaptations vs source books; the parent steps back from narrator to "book buddy".

**Six-dimension mapping**: **general knowledge** (*A Really Short History of Nearly Everything*) + **language** (English originals, e.g., *Harry Potter* 1) + values discussion (*The Little Prince*, *The Straw House* below).

- **《小王子》The Little Prince** (Antoine de Saint-Exupéry)｜Topic: love & responsibility ｜Builds: fable-style reflection, philosophical lines
  - Tip: reread it yourself first; discuss exactly one question per week ("What does 'tame' mean?") — an open question left hanging matters more than "getting the whole book".
- **《城南旧事》Memories of Peking: South Side Stories** (Hai-yin Lin; English translation published by Chinese Literature Press — availability varies; pair with **[substitute] Inside Out and Back Again** by Thanhha Lai, a verse-novel childhood memoir, if unobtainable)｜Topic: childhood & parting ｜Builds: realistic literary language, historical context
  - Tip: watch a clip of the 1983 film first for setting; after each chapter the child writes a one-line chapter title of their own.
- **《哈利·波特》Harry Potter series** (J.K. Rowling)｜Topic: fantasy adventure ｜Builds: long-series stamina, complex characters & moral judgment
  - Entry volume: *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's/Sorcerer's Stone*; read in order (foreshadowing spans volumes); parallel-read the first three together, then pace vol. 4+ (darker, longer) by the child's tolerance.
  - Tip: after each volume, watch its film and build a "best scene the movie cut" list — natural critical-reading practice.
- **《草房子》The Straw House** (Wenxuan Cao) — an English translation exists but circulation is limited; if unavailable, **[substitute] Where the Mountain Meets the Moon** (Grace Lin) for lyrical Chinese-culture-rooted fiction｜Topic: hardship & dignity ｜Builds: literary aesthetic, empathy
  - Tip: each character owns a chapter — the child picks "who I'd most / least want to be" and defends the choice.
- **《万物简史（少儿版）》A Really Short History of Nearly Everything** (Bill Bryson)｜Topic: science overview ｜Builds: **general knowledge**, cross-domain interest mapping
  - Method: don't read in order — start from the child's favorite chapters (dinosaurs/space/the body); pair "10 nonfiction pages a day + unlimited fiction" to protect interest.
- **Anne of Green Gables** (L.M. Montgomery)｜Topic: the power of imagination ｜Builds: character-arc tracking, descriptive writing
  - Tip: whenever Anne renames a place ("the White Way of Delight"), the child renames three spots on the school run.
- **《海底两万里》Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea** (Jules Verne)｜Topic: classic science fiction ｜Builds: the science-fantasy frontier, a hard-mode reading challenge
  - Method: choose a full translation, not an abridgment; after each chapter, watch a matching ocean clip to cross-check the science.
