# Parent Communication Playbook

> Knowledge layer | Language: en mirror (source: `knowledge/communication-playbook.md`) | Type: script toolkit
> Follows DESIGN.md §1.3 (no judgment, no anxiety creation) and §8 (actionable, neutral, science-based).
> How to use: these scripts are "worth trying first," not the only right answer — adjust to your child's temperament and the moment.

## The Five-Step Communication Framework

> Sources: *How to Talk So Kids Will Listen…*（《如何说孩子才会听》）, *No-Drama Discipline*（《去情绪化管教》）, *The Whole-Brain Child*（《全脑教养法》）, *Nonviolent Communication*（《非暴力沟通》）, *Positive Discipline*（《正面管教》）

### Step 1: Catch the emotion (connect before you correct)

> Sources: *No-Drama Discipline*, *The Whole-Brain Child* — mid-storm, the rational brain is temporarily "offline"; name the feeling and calm the body first.

- ❌ "There's nothing to cry about!"
  ✅ "You're really sad — the tower falling made you angry." (Name the emotion, *The Whole-Brain Child*)
- ❌ "Stop that tantrum or I'm leaving you here."
  ✅ "I can see you're really angry. I'm right here when you're ready to talk." (Connection, *No-Drama Discipline*)
- ❌ "Not this again. So annoying."
  ✅ "Your feeling got too big to hold. Let's take three slow breaths together." (Calm the body, *No-Drama Discipline*)

### Step 2: Describe the facts (no labels, no history)

> Sources: *How to Talk…*, *Nonviolent Communication* — describe what you see and leave the fixing to the child.

- ❌ "Why do you always spill water everywhere!"
  ✅ "I see water on the table." (Describe, don't accuse, *How to Talk…*)
- ❌ "You're such a scatterbrain."
  ✅ "Your backpack is by the door; your water bottle is still on the coffee table." (Observation, not judgment, *Nonviolent Communication*)
- ❌ "I've told you a hundred times!"
  ✅ "Shoes." (One word beats a lecture, *How to Talk…*)

### Step 3: Limited choices (both acceptable; give a sense of control)

> Source: *Positive Discipline* — small decisions belong to the child; this defuses power struggles.

- ❌ "Get dressed now, stop dawdling!"
  ✅ "The red one or the blue one — you pick."
- ❌ "Bath. Now."
  ✅ "Do you want your bath now, or after you finish this puzzle — five minutes?"
- ❌ "Turn it off. No discussion."
  ✅ "When this episode ends — do you want to turn it off yourself, or together with me?" (Pair with advance warning, see scenarios)

### Step 4: Solve it together (hand the problem back)

> Sources: *P.E.T.*, *How to Talk…* — plans the child helps create meet far less resistance.

- ❌ "No more grabbing your sister's things. Got it?"
  ✅ "You both want this truck. Let's think of ideas — can you come up with one?" (No-lose negotiation, *P.E.T.*)
- ❌ "I don't care how — no more being late."
  ✅ "Mornings keep going wrong. Let's list why, then pick one idea to try tomorrow?" (Problem-solving steps, *How to Talk…*)
- ❌ "I said no. That's final."
  ✅ "I won't buy it now (limit). We can take a photo for the birthday wish list (outlet)." (Limit + outlet, *Good Inside*)

### Step 5: Descriptive praise (praise process and specifics, not "smart")

> Sources: *How to Talk…*, *Positive Discipline* — descriptive praise shows the child exactly what worked, so it can be repeated.

- ❌ "You're so smart!"
  ✅ "The tower fell three times and you didn't give up — the fourth time you built it yourself." (Describe effort)
- ❌ "What a good boy!"
  ✅ "You saw your sister crying and handed her the toy — then she stopped." (Describe behavior + effect)
- ❌ "Amazing! Great picture!"
  ✅ "You used three colors for the sky. Tell me about this picture?" (Describe detail + invite expression)

## High-Frequency Scenario Scripts

> Each script cites its source book. Try one first, watch your child's response, then adjust.

### Scenario 1: Waking up grumpy

- "Good morning, you're so grumpy today. Come lean on me and wake up slowly." (Catch the emotion, *No-Drama Discipline*)
- "Coat first or teeth first?" (Limited choices, *Positive Discipline*)
- "Let's change how you wake up: today do you get woken by the Sock Monster, or by tickles?" (Play, *Playful Parenting*)
- "I carried the breakfast smell over. Sniff — what is it?" (Sensory wake-up, *Playful Parenting*)

### Scenario 2: Refusing to turn off the TV

- (Warn ahead) "Five more minutes. When this episode ends — you turn it off yourself, or together with me?" (Warning + choice, *Positive Discipline*)
- "I know this episode is so good; turning it off is hard." (Catch the emotion, *The Whole-Brain Child*)
- "You don't want it off (feeling), **and** we agreed on one episode (limit). Let's put it on the 'watch tomorrow first' list." (Feeling + limit + outlet, *Good Inside*)
- "What does the note next to the TV say again?" (Point to the pre-posted agreement note, *How to Talk…*)

### Scenario 3: Hitting / biting

- "I won't let you hit (limit). You can be angry — I'll stay with you." (Limit + presence, *Good Inside*)
- (Once calm) "What happened? How did you feel? What could you do next time?" (Three reflective questions, *No-Drama Discipline*)
- "When you're too angry, you can hit this pillow, or come squeeze me tight." (Acceptable outlet, *No-Drama Discipline*)
- "Jake's arm got hit — it hurts and he's sad. What could you do to help him feel better?" (Guide repair, *No-Drama Discipline*)
- "You really wanted that toy. Say it with words: 'Can I have a turn?' Let's practice once." (Teach the replacement skill, *How to Talk…*)

### Scenario 4: Meltdown in public (store / mall)

- Kneel, lower your voice: "You want that so much, and not getting it is really hard. I'm staying right here with you." (Connect first, shrink the scene, *No-Drama Discipline*)
- "I'm not buying this (limit). We can take a photo and put it on the wish list (outlet)." (Limit + outlet, *Good Inside*)
- (Rehearse before leaving) "If you see candy you really want, we take a photo for the list — we're not buying today. Tell me the plan back." (Scripting, *Good Inside*)
- "We need to leave. Walk by yourself, or hold my hand?" (A limited choice that preserves dignity, *Positive Discipline*)

### Scenario 5: Refusing to eat

- "Dinner is served until 7. Table clears at 7 — you decide how much in between." (Control within limits, *Positive Discipline*)
- "This meal might not be your favorite. Is there one thing on the plate you'd try a bite of first?" (Lower the bar + choice, *Positive Discipline*)
- "Broccoli is a little tree and you're a dinosaur — treetop first or trunk?" (Playful, *Playful Parenting*)
- No chasing or coaxing: "You don't seem hungry. Go play — see you at the next meal." (Natural consequence, calm execution, *Positive Discipline*)
- (Debrief after, not at the table) "What didn't work about dinner tonight? What would you like added tomorrow?" (Listening debrief, *P.E.T.*)

### Scenario 6: Separation anxiety (drop-off / leaving)

- "You'll miss me and I'll miss you. Here's a 'kiss in the palm' — squeeze it whenever you miss me." (Ritualized connection, *Playful Parenting*)
- "One hug, one kiss, one wave — then Mommy goes. When you miss me, you can touch the charm on your backpack." (A short, predictable goodbye — no lingering)
- "Three hugs while we're apart, and dinner brings me back. Look — that's the time on the clock." (Visible time + reunion preview, *Hold Back! Don't Step In*)
- "Missing me means you love me. Your teacher will take care of you, and I'll be here at 4 o'clock sharp." (Name the feeling + a certain promise, *The Whole-Brain Child*)

### Scenario 7: Toy-grabbing (siblings or playmates)

- "You both want to play with it. Let's think of ideas: turns? trading? playing together? Which one?" (Solve together, *P.E.T.*)
- "We flip the hourglass — when it runs out, you swap." (A tool instead of a judge, *Hold Back! Don't Step In*)
- "I know you weren't done, and having it taken felt urgent. You can say: 'I'm not done yet — 3 more minutes.'" (Teach the sentence, *How to Talk…*)
- "The truck is Jake's and he's taking it home. You can ask: 'Can I borrow it again tomorrow?'" (Ownership + request phrasing, *How to Talk…*)

### Scenario 8: "I hate you!"

- "You are so, so angry right now (translation: the feeling is too big — it isn't the whole truth). I'm here." (Behavior is a signal, *Good Inside*)
- "You hate that I stopped your game — not me." (Separate the person from the act, *The Whole-Brain Child*)
- "Even when you hate me, I still love you. Let's talk once we're both calm." (Steady inside the limit, *Good Inside*)
- (At the debrief) "When you said you hated me — what did you actually want? More play, or more of me?" (Look for the need, *Nonviolent Communication*)

### Scenario 9: Afraid of the dark / won't sleep

- "The dark room feels like it has scary things in it. Let's keep the night-light on and you tell me what it looks like — then draw it." (Externalize the fear, *The Whole-Brain Child*)
- "Give it a name and tell it: 'You may not scare me. My mom is right next door.'" (Empowering script, *Playful Parenting*)
- "In the daytime we play flashlight treasure hunt — and at night you're the explorer holding the flashlight." (Role reversal, *Playful Parenting*)
- "You can always call me when you're scared. Before bed we do the same routine: five breaths and a ten-second hug." (A fixed routine = safety, *No-Drama Discipline*)

### Scenario 10: Dawdling out the door

- "Which two boxes on the going-out list are still empty?" (Point to the routine chart instead of nagging, *Hold Back! Don't Step In*)
- "Timer set for five minutes — dressed before the bell wins the round. Ready — go!" (Timer + play, *1-2-3 Magic*, *Playful Parenting*)
- "Walk to the car yourself, or shall I 'transport' you — airplane style or kangaroo style?" (Playful finish, *Playful Parenting*)
- (When repeats fail) "I'll count to 3 and we go out — walk yourself, or hold my hand?" (Count + choice, *1-2-3 Magic*, *Positive Discipline*)
- (Debrief later, not mid-scene) "We were late this morning. Let's re-arrange the morning — how could it go faster?" (Solve together, *P.E.T.*)

## Phrase Whitelist (recommended habits)

> Goal: leave room for uncertainty and negotiation; reduce judgment and labeling.

| Recommended phrase | What it does | Source |
|--------------------|--------------|--------|
| "It's more likely that…" | Speculation over verdict — "He's more likely tired than naughty" | *Good Inside* (behavior-as-signal) |
| "Worth trying first…" | Lowers the pressure of the method; allows adjustment | *No-Drama Discipline* |
| "You're expressing a need" | Translates behavior into need — for your child and yourself | *Nonviolent Communication* |
| "I notice that…" | Opens with observation, not judgment | *Nonviolent Communication* |
| "…and…" | The kind-and-firm construction: "I love you **and** we're not buying it" | *Positive Discipline* |
| "What do you need from me?" | Consultant-style support; hands back initiative | *The Self-Driven Child* |
| "Tell me more" | A door-opener for listening | *P.E.T.* |

## Phrase Blacklist (never use + replacements)

> Goal: remove conditional love, comparisons, motive-labeling, and emotion-suppression. If a phrase has already been said, a repair conversation can redo it (see the repair script from *Good Inside* below).

| Never say | Why | Replace with |
|-----------|-----|--------------|
| "If you keep this up, Mommy won't love you" | Turns love into a bargaining chip; makes security conditional | "I love you, **and** this isn't okay. We can be angry and we can figure it out." (*Positive Discipline*) |
| "Every other kid can do this" | Comparison creates shame and offers no method | "Last month you couldn't tie your shoes — now you can. We'll start this one step by step too." (Compare to their own past, *The Self-Driven Child*) |
| "You did that on purpose" | Assumes bad intent and closes the conversation | "I'd like to know what happened just now." (Inquiry over verdict, *No-Drama Discipline*) |
| "Boys don't cry" | Suppresses expression; the emotion doesn't disappear, it deforms | "It's okay to cry. When you're done, we'll figure it out." (Catch the emotion, *The Whole-Brain Child*) |
| "You're so clumsy / such a scaredy-cat" | Personality labels get internalized as identity | "This is still too hard — let's break it into smaller steps." (Action, not identity, *Positive Discipline*) |
| "1… 2… 3!" (shouted as a threat) | High-pressure intimidation; the child only learns fear | Counting itself is fine — but only with a calm voice and a pre-agreed consequence (*1-2-3 Magic*), never shouted as a threat |
| "Fine, whatever. I'm done." | Withdrawal is cold violence | "I'm very angry and need two minutes to calm down — then we'll talk." (Parent time-out, *No-Drama Discipline*) |

## Praise and Criticism

### Descriptive praise vs. empty praise

> Sources: *How to Talk…*, *Positive Discipline*. Formula: **describe what you see + describe the feeling/effect**, optionally summed up in one word.

| Empty praise ❌ | Descriptive praise ✅ |
|----------------|------------------------|
| "You're so smart!" | "You found a different way to solve that problem." |
| "Great job!" | "You put the markers back in the drawer — the table is clear and things are easy to find." |
| "What a good kid!" | "You waited while I was on the phone, and you drew this picture while waiting." |
| "Perfect! Genius!" | "You practiced three times and now you can button it yourself — that's called 'practice pays off.'" (One-word close) |

Key points:
- Praise **effort, strategy, and progress** — not talent ("smart," "gifted"). Talent-praised children start avoiding challenge (*The Self-Driven Child*, stress lens).
- Praise **specific behavior** and its **effect on others/the room** so the child knows exactly what to repeat (*How to Talk…*).

### Criticism: address the act, not the person

> Sources: *No-Drama Discipline*, *Nonviolent Communication*. Formula: **describe the behavior + state the impact/feeling + point to repair or next step**.

- ❌ "You're such a selfish child!"
  ✅ "Sister's picture got torn (behavior), and she cried hard (impact). Let's think how to help her feel better (repair)."
- ❌ "You always knock your cup over!"
  ✅ "Water spilled on the table (behavior) and the notebook got wet (impact). The cloth is by the sink — you wipe it or we do it together (next step)?"
- ❌ "Liar! You're a liar!"
  ✅ "You said 8 o'clock, and the watch says 9 (observation). I'd like to know why (inquiry) — were you afraid I'd be angry? We can say it again."
- Timing: no criticism mid-storm — wait for calm; one incident at a time; no dredging up history (*No-Drama Discipline*).
- Parent repair template: "I yelled at you earlier / said something hurtful. That wasn't your fault — I didn't manage my own feelings. I'd like a hug and a restart." (*Good Inside*)
