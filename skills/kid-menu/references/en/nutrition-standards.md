## 5. Limits and Safety Red Lines

| Age band | Salt | Added sugar | Other limits and red lines |
|----------|------|-------------|---------------------------|
| 0-12m | **No salt** or any seasonings | **No added sugar** | **Honey FORBIDDEN (≤12 months — infant botulism risk)**; no juice or sugar-sweetened drinks; no cow's milk/low-fat milk as the main drink; no whole nuts, jelly cups, whole grapes/cherry tomatoes or other choking-hazard foods (see `references/safety-rules.json`) |
| 1-2y | None or as little as possible (<1.5 g/day, reference value) | None | Choking-hazard foods still restricted (except honey-based items above); juice not recommended — offer whole fruit |
| 2-3y | <2 g/day | Avoid if possible, <10 g/day (reference value) | Nuts must be ground; grapes/cherry tomatoes quartered; continue to follow the choking-foods list in `references/safety-rules.json` |
| 4-6y | <3 g/day | <25 g/day (WHO: free sugars <10%E, ideally <5%E) | Limit sugary drinks, candied fruit, heavily processed snacks |
| 7-10y | <4 g/day | <25 g/day | Limit sugary drinks and high-salt snacks; beware cheap street snacks near schools |
| 11-12y | <5 g/day | <25 g/day | Limit bubble tea/energy drinks; energy drinks not recommended for children |

> **Safety red lines (mandatory, all ages)**:
> 1. **Honey is absolutely prohibited under 1 year of age** (infant botulism risk).
> 2. For children under 3, the choking-hazard food list (whole nuts, jelly cups, whole grapes/cherry tomatoes, large hard pieces, whole pitted dates, etc.) is governed by `references/safety-rules.json`; menu generation must filter or re-cut these items.
> 3. For allergic children, ingredients matching profile `allergies[]` are absolutely excluded (with cross-contamination alerts) — see design doc §6.3 Safety Filter.

Sources: Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents (2022) / WHO guidance on free sugars and sodium intake. Provided for reference only.

## 6. Feeding Principle Essentials

1. **Responsive feeding**: feed in response to the child's hunger and fullness cues; never force-feed, chase-feed, or use food as the sole reward or comfort; treat feeding as warm parent-child interaction with patience and encouragement.
2. **Dietary diversity**: reach 4+ food groups daily (grains, vegetables, fruits, animal foods/legumes) and 25+ distinct foods weekly (reference value); expand variety starting from the complementary-food stage.
3. **Iron-fortified foods first**: from 6 months, prioritize iron-fortified infant cereal, red meat purée, and liver purée to reduce iron-deficiency anemia risk.
4. **Low salt, low sugar, plain complementary food**: no salt or sugar in the first year; cultivate a light palate throughout childhood.
5. **Milk priority**: breast milk/formula is primary under 1 year; 400-600 ml milk at 1-2y; 300 ml+ for preschool and school age, with variety from yogurt and cheese.
6. **Eating autonomy and mealtime environment**: fixed seating, regular meals (3 meals + 2 snacks for preschoolers); allow and encourage self-feeding with hands/utensils; no TV or screens during meals.
7. **Continue breastfeeding to 2 years of age or beyond** (WHO recommendation, respecting mother's and child's wishes).
8. **Pair with physical activity**: keep eating and activity on a regular rhythm; no vigorous activity immediately after meals.

Sources: Core recommendations of the Infant & Young Child Feeding Guidelines in Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents (2022) / WHO infant and young child feeding recommendations.

## 7. Special Pattern Quick Reference

### 7.1 Bland diet (illness period / health_mode=care)

- **Ingredient points**: easily digestible congee, soft noodles, steamed egg custard, winter melon, pumpkin, carrot, apple purée/stewed apple; low-fat easily digestible proteins (egg, tofu, small amounts of chicken or fish purée); avoid fried foods, high-fiber coarse grains, raw/cold foods, high sugar and fat.
- **Cooking points**: steam, boil, or braise; make food one texture level softer than the age-appropriate standard; hydrate in small frequent amounts (oral rehydration salts per physician advice); rebuild protein and energy gradually during recovery, never force-feed.
- **Red-line reminder**: frequent vomiting/diarrhea, lethargy, or persistent fever trigger urgent-care criteria — see `references/safety-rules.json`.

### 7.2 Iron-boost pattern (iron-deficiency risk / anemia recovery, reference)

- **Ingredient points**: prioritize heme iron — beef purée, pork purée, pork/chicken liver purée (liver 1-2x per week, 5-10 g per serving, reference value), duck blood; non-heme iron — iron-fortified cereal, wood-ear mushroom, leafy greens.
- **Cooking points**: pair red meat with vitamin C in the same meal (e.g., tomato beef stew, bell-pepper liver purée) to boost absorption; do NOT take tea, coffee, or large amounts of milk **with** iron-rich meals (they inhibit absorption); iron supplements must follow a physician's prescription — never self-supplement.

### 7.3 Calcium-boost pattern

- **Ingredient points**: dairy first (breast milk/formula/milk/yogurt/cheese); gypsum-firm tofu, sesame paste, leafy greens (baby bok choy, shepherd's purse), small soft-boned fish and dried shrimp (prepared per age).
- **Cooking points**: ensure vitamin D alongside (daily 400 IU supplement + outdoor activity); blanch high-oxalate greens (e.g., spinach) before cooking; take calcium supplements separately from milk (reference value), per physician advice.

### 7.4 Quick-meal pattern (low parent_time_daily_min)

- **Ingredient points**: frozen vegetable cubes (corn/peas/carrots), eggs, ready-to-eat tofu, tomatoes, noodles/frozen low-sodium dumplings, no-cook fruits like bananas/apples.
- **Cooking points**: one-pot dishes (vegetable & minced-meat braised noodles, tomato egg noodles); weekend batch-prep of frozen purée cubes (meat/vegetable purée, use within 2 weeks, reference value); rice cooker delayed-start congee; every meal must cover "staple + protein + vegetable"; ready in 10-20 minutes.
- **Safety reminders**: thawed purée must never be re-frozen; even quick meals must filter allergens and choking-hazard ingredients.

Sources: Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents (2022), all volumes. Provided for reference only; individual variation — follow medical advice.
